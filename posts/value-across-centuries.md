## The Art Corner: The Night Watch

**Object number SK-C-5, Rijksmuseum — painted in 1642.**

The museum catalogs Rembrandt's masterpiece — formally *Het korporaalschap van kapitein Frans Banninck Cocq en luitenant Willem van Ruytenburch, bekend als de 'Nachtwacht'* — under object number **SK-C-5**, and dates it to **1642**. Both come straight from the museum's own collection record, which is the standard we hold this digest to.

Key facts from the record:

- **Year: 1642** — the confirmed date on the museum's official record for SK-C-5.
- **It's a militia company portrait, not a night scene.** The full catalog title names the subjects: Captain Frans Banninck Cocq's civic guard company and Lieutenant Willem van Ruytenburch. The "night" is an accident of darkened varnish, not of design.
- **Oil on canvas.** The museum's collection data classifies the work's material in the record's `made_of` field — oil paint (olieverf) — rather than tempera or a panel. The record also carries structured dimensions data for the work; for the exact centimeter figures we defer to the museum's own collection page for SK-C-5, that's the authoritative source, and we'd rather send you there than paraphrase it.
- **Pulled from the museum's machine-readable record, not a screenshot.** The record resolves to object number SK-C-5 (record id https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200107928), confirms the 1642 date, and carries structured materials and dimensions fields. That object is one of the museum's toppieces in the collection data services.

## A Career, in Dated Works

To show how one painter's value compounded over a lifetime, here is a short chronology of Rembrandt's career drawn from the Rijksmuseum's own collection data — dated objects spanning nearly forty years:

| Year | Work | Object number |
|------|------|---------------|
| 1626 | *Allegorisch musicerend gezelschap* | SK-A-4674 |
| 1635 | *An oriental* | SK-A-3340 |
| 1638 | *De stenen brug* | SK-A-1935 |
| 1642 | *The Night Watch* | SK-C-5 |
| 1660 | *De verloochening van Petrus* | SK-A-3137 |
| 1662 | *De Staalmeesters: the Amsterdam lakenbekers' college of weights and measures* | SK-C-6 |
| 1665 | *The Jewish Bride* | SK-C-216 |

Seven dated objects in the museum's data for this artist, from an allegorical music scene of 1626 through *The Jewish Bride* of 1665 — a career that ran nearly four decades. The museum's collection service also returned a further dated work, SK-A-3981 from 1639, whose record did not surface a title in this pull.

Read down that column of years. In 1626 Rembrandt is painting allegorical music scenes. In 1642 he paints the Night Watch. In 1660 he paints *The Casting Out of Peter* — the artist who would, four years later, sell off his own collection and possessions to pay his creditors — and in 1662 *The Syndics*, and in 1665, *The Jewish Bride*. The market priced him low in his final decade. The record, four centuries later, disagrees. That's the long end of the compounding curve.
