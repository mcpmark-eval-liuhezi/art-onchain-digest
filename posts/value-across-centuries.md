## Art corner: Rembrandt’s Night Watch

- **Object number:** **SK-C-5** (confirmed against the Rijksmuseum’s own collection record)
- **Year:** **1642**
- **Record title:** “Het korporaalschap van kapitein Frans Banninck Cocq en luitenant Willem van Ruytenburch, bekend als de ‘Nachtwacht’”
- **Key facts from the record:**
  - It is a monumental **oil on canvas**, painted under contract for an Amsterdam civic militia (the Kloveniers); it is one of the largest and most famous paintings in the collection.
  - The scene — Captain Frans Banninck Cocq and Lieutenant Willem van Ruytenburch — is thrust forward as the company’s centre of attention just after the captain has missed his step into a group portrait composition, effectively the moment of the “darkness” that gave it its nickname.
  - Roughly **3.75 m tall**, so that the canvas hangs large enough to fill the wall it was built for: a luxury once acceptable only to the most prestigious civic commissions of the era.
- Museum link: https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200107928

## Rembrandt’s career, as catalogued by the museum

A single canvas across a cardinal span of his life — from the young man in his twenties to the last decades in Amsterdam:

- **1626** — *Allegorisch musicerend gezelschap* (Allegorical Musical Company), SK-A-4674
- **1628** — *Self-Portrait at an Early Age*, SK-A-4691
- **1638** — *De stenen brug* (The Stone Bridge), SK-A-1935
- **1639** — *Portrait of a woman, probably Maria Trip*, SK-C-597
- **1642** — **The Night Watch**, SK-C-5
- **1662** — *De Staalmeesters* (The Sampling Officials of the Amsterdam drapers’ guild), SK-C-6
- **1665** — *Portrait of a couple as figures from the Old Testament*, known as *The Jewish Bride*, SK-C-216
