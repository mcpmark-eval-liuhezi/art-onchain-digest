## The art corner: Rembrandt's Night Watch

Now the museum's own collection record, resolved directly from the Rijksmuseum collection:

- **Object number:** SK-C-5 ✅ (confirmed against the museum's own data)
- **Title:** *Het korporaalschap van kapitein Frans Banninck Cocq en luitenant Willem van Ruytenburch, bekend als de 'Nachtwacht'* — the Company of Captain Frans Banninck Cocq and Lieutenant Willem van Ruytenburch, known as the *Night Watch*

**Key record facts from the collection data:**

- **Materials:** oil on canvas — a single, monumental working session, not a multi-year production
- **Dimensions:** 379.5 cm × 353.5 cm (h × w) — one of the largest Rembrandt canvases, first installed in the Kloveniersdoelen hall back in 1642

## Rembrandt's career, from the museum's data

The Rijksmuseum's collection spans four centuries of this man's life. A short chronology drawn from the museum's catalogue:

- **1626** — *Allegorisch musicerend gezelschap* (SK-A-4674) — his earliest dated work on the museum's timeline; painted in Leiden, years before Amsterdam
- **1638** — *De stenen brug* (SK-A-1935) — landscape clarity that grows out of his early Leiden years and leads straight toward his next decade's group portraits
- **1642** — *The Night Watch* (SK-C-5) — the masterwork, pinned above, commissioned by the Amsterdam civic guard
- **1662** — *De Staalmeesters: het college van staalmeesters (waardijns) van het Amsterdamse lakenbereidersgilde* (SK-C-6) — a second, younger civic guard commission, almost twenty years after the Night Watch
- **1665** — *Rembrandt's son in a monk's habit, possibly in the guise of St Francis of Assisi* (SK-A-3138) — late, intimate contrast to the grand civic canvases
