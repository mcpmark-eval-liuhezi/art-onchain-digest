## Art Corner: The Night Watch

The centerpiece is Rembrandt's **Night Watch**, catalogued by the Rijksmuseum under object number **SK-C-5**.

- **Title:** Het korporaalschap van kapitein Frans Banninck Cocq en luitenant Willem van Ruytenburch, bekend als de 'Nachtwacht'
- **Object number:** SK-C-5
- **Year:** 1642
- **Materials:** oil on canvas
- **Dimensions:** approx. 379.5 cm × 469.5 cm (roughly 3.8 m × 4.7 m), one of the largest paintings in the museum's collection

The record places the work at https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200107928 (Rijksmuseum collection ID).

## Rembrandt Career Chronology (from museum data)

A short chronology of dated works by Rembrandt van Rijn held in the Rijksmuseum collection:

1. **Allegorisch musicerend gezelschap** (Allegorical musical company) — 1626, SK-A-4674
2. **Portrait of Haesje Jacobsdr van Cleyburg** (wife of the Rotterdam brewer Dirck Jansz Pesser) — 1634, SK-A-4833
3. **An oriental** — 1635, SK-A-3340
4. **De stenen brug** (The Stone Bridge) — 1638, SK-A-1935
5. **SK-A-3981** — 1639, SK-A-3981
6. **The Night Watch** (Nachtwacht) — 1642, SK-C-5
7. **De verloochening van Petrus** (The Denial of St Peter) — 1660, SK-A-3137
8. **Rembrandt's son in a monk's habit, possibly as St Francis of Assisi** — 1660, SK-A-3138
9. **De Staalmeesters** (The Sampling Officials) — 1662, SK-C-6
10. **Portrait of a couple as figures from the Old Testament, known as 'The Jewish Bride'** — 1665, SK-C-216

The chronology spans from Rembrandt's early Leiden years (1626 allegorical piece) through the Amsterdam master period, culminating in late works such as The Jewish Bride (1665).

## The Bridge

Four centuries apart: a canvas sized to hold a militia company in motion, and a network sized to hold trust between strangers. In 1642 Rembrandt used light, shadow and gesture to freeze a moment; in 2026 Chainlink uses tokens and oracles to freeze value at a timestamp readers can verify. One painting anchored the reputation of an entire civic militia; the other anchors the price of an entire network. Both are catalogued — one by a museum, the other by a feed — and both resist being worth less than their recorded date suggests.
