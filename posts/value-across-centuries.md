## A chronology of Rembrandt's career (Rijksmuseum data)

- **1626 — *Allegorical Musical Company* (SK-A-4674):** Early Leiden work, turning outward to genres and history subjects.
- **1634 — *Portrait of Haesje Jacobsdr van Cleyburg* (SK-A-4833):** Amsterdam portrait period, sitter fully dressed in contemporary attire.
- **1635 — *An Oriental* (SK-A-3340):** Roomy figure study in dark ground, typical of the 1630s Amsterdam success years.
- **1638 — *The Stone Bridge* (SK-A-1935):** Landscape with luminous water and reflections.
- **1642 — *The Night Watch* (SK-C-5):** The great turning point; the militia group broken into dynamic movement rather than static poses.
- **1660 — *The Denial of Saint Peter* (SK-A-3137) and *Son in a Monk's Habit* (SK-A-3138):** Late-career religious and portrait studies after personal and artistic trials.
- **1662 — *The Sampling Officials* (SK-C-6):** One of the two great Amsterdam civic-guard group pictorials.
- **1665 — *The Jewish Bride* (SK-C-216):** Late masterpiece of compressed masses and golden tonality.

## Verdict

Four centuries apart, the common thread is commission, craft, and market: the Guild of Artillery paid for the Night Watch the year it was finished; today the recipe for trust in art — and in art's price — is increasingly onchain.
