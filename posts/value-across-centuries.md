## Art corner: The Night Watch

The museum's own collection record confirms the centerpiece of this week's digest: Rembrandt's *Night Watch*, cataloged under object number **SK-C-5**.

- **Object number:** SK-C-5
- **Title (as cataloged):** *Het korporaalschap van kapitein Frans Banninck Cocq en luitenant Willem van Ruytenburch, bekend als de 'Nachtwacht'* (The Militia Company of Captain Frans Banninck Cocq and Lieutenant Willem van Ruytenburch, known as the 'Night Watch')
- **Year:** 1642
- **Materials:** oil on canvas
- **Dimensions:** 379.5 cm × 353.5 cm (about 149 in × 139 in)
- **Location:** Amsterdam, Rijksmuseum (SK-C-5)
- **Record reference:** Rijksmuseum collection record, `https://id.rijksmuseum.nl/200107928`

Slapped together as one painted machine of light and shadow, the work commissioned by an Amsterdam militia company is widely credited with ending the era of static group portraits and launching the dynamic Dutch history scene. Four years after it was finished, Rembrandt was in debt, and the painting found itself in a music room that was too dark to hang it properly in.

## Reading the runway

Three and a half centuries laid end to end — from the young Rembrandt's allegorical music company to the Jewish Bride — and over that same span, the value of a single work moved from a militia commission to the national icon that anchors its own gallery today. Onchain, the same ledger principle applies: a dollar kind of weight against metrology, whether you trust it or price it out — but here, twice over, the Euro and a painting, both carry weight.
